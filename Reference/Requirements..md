**Means Test Requirements for the Debtor**

The means test (under 11 U.S.C. § 707(b)) determines if a Chapter 7 filing is an "abuse" of the system for individuals with primarily consumer debts. It does **not** apply to Chapter 13 filers in the same way but informs the "disposable income" calculation for their repayment plan (via Official Form 122C series).

* **Who must complete it?**  
  All individual debtors filing Chapter 7 must submit means test forms (Official Forms 122A-1, 122A-1Supp if exempt, and 122A-2 if needed). However, if your "current monthly income" (average gross income over the 6 full calendar months before filing) is **at or below** the California median for your household size, you are presumed eligible for Chapter 7—no full means test calculation is required (you skip Form 122A-2).  
  * If above median → Proceed to full means test: Deduct allowed expenses (IRS standards \+ actuals for certain items) to calculate disposable income. Abuse is presumed if projected 60-month disposable income exceeds the lesser of (i) 25% of nonpriority unsecured debt or $10,275 (whichever greater), or (ii) $17,150 (figures adjusted periodically; current as of recent updates).  
* **Timing and Filing**  
  The means test is calculated and forms filed **with the petition** (pre-filing preparation). Debtors provide income data (e.g., pay stubs for last 6 months, tax returns). The test uses Census Bureau/IRS data for medians and standards—updated \~every 6 months (e.g., November/May). For cases filed on/after November 1, 2025 (relevant to current 2026 timing), check justice.gov/ust/means-testing for exact California medians (e.g., around $77k–$135k+ for 1–4 person households, varying by update).  
* **Exceptions**  
  * Disabled veterans, active military in certain zones, or cases involving primarily non-consumer debts may be exempt.  
  * Courts can still find abuse under "totality of circumstances" even if passing the test.

For Chapter 13, the means test determines plan length (36 vs. 60 months) and minimum payments but does not bar filing.

  The means test is calculated and forms filed **with the petition** (pre-filing preparation). Debtors provide income data (e.g., pay stubs for last 6 months, tax returns). The test uses Census Bureau/IRS data for medians and standards—updated \~every 6 months (e.g., November/May). For cases filed on/after November 1, 2025 (relevant to current 2026 timing), check justice.gov/ust/means-testing for exact California medians (e.g., around $77k–$135k+ for 1–4 person households, varying by update).  
* **Exceptions**  
  * Disabled veterans, active military in certain zones, or cases involving primarily non-consumer debts may be exempt.  
  * Courts can still find abuse under "totality of circumstances" even if passing the test.

For Chapter 13, the means test determines plan length (36 vs. 60 months) and minimum payments but does not bar filing.

**Meetings/Interactions with Counsel or Assistants (Pre- and Post-Filing)**

There are **no federal statutory requirements** mandating that a debtor meet in person (or at all) with an attorney or legal assistant before or after filing—bankruptcy is available pro se (self-represented) for individuals.

* **Pre-Filing**  
  No required meeting with counsel/assistants. However:  
  * Debtors must complete **pre-filing credit counseling** from a U.S. Trustee-approved agency (individual or group briefing, often online/phone, within 180 days before filing). Certificate filed with petition.  
  * Practical reality: Most attorneys require consultations (often 1–several meetings/calls) to gather info, run means test, prepare forms, and advise on Chapter 7 vs. 13\. Pro se filers skip this but risk errors/dismissal. Courts strongly recommend attorney help due to complexity.  
* **Post-Filing**  
  * **Mandatory 341 Meeting of Creditors** (§ 341(a)): All individual debtors **must attend** (in person or sometimes virtual/telephonic per court/trustee rules). Held 21–50 days after filing, presided by trustee (not judge). Debtor answers questions under oath about assets, debts, income, etc. Bring photo ID, SSN proof, recent pay stubs, bank statements, etc. Attorney (if retained) attends to assist/represent but does not answer for you. Failure to attend usually results in case dismissal. Creditors may attend/ask questions but rarely do.  
  * **Post-filing debtor education** (personal financial management course): Required after filing but before discharge (from approved provider; certificate filed). Often online, 2+ hours. No required attorney involvement.  
  * Additional trustee requests: May need follow-up document production or meetings (e.g., for asset questions in Chapter 7).

In summary:

* **Means test** → Debtor must provide data and file forms pre-petition; below-median income simplifies it significantly.  
* **Counsel/assistant meetings** → Not legally required (pre or post), but the 341 meeting is mandatory debtor attendance, and credit/debtor education courses are required (pre- and post-filing, respectively).
